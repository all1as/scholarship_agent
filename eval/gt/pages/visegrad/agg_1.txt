# Visegrad Scholarship Program

A Master's and post-Master's mobility scholarship funded by the International Visegrad Fund, supporting one to two semesters of study or research at accredited universities across the Visegrad Group (Czechia, Hungary, Poland, Slovakia) and partner countries in Eastern Europe and the Western Balkans.

## About the Visegrad Scholarship Program

🕐 4 min read

The Visegrad Scholarship Program is run by the International Visegrad Fund, an organization jointly financed by the governments of Czechia, Hungary, Poland, and Slovakia (the 'V4') to support regional cooperation. The scholarship funds Master's-level and post-Master's (research/postdoctoral) study for one to two semesters at more than 190 accredited universities and research institutions across the V4 countries and a defined set of neighbouring partner countries.

2

The program runs through two main mobility directions. The first, 'Intra-Visegrad', supports citizens of one V4 country studying in another V4 country. The second, and more relevant for students outside the immediate region, supports bidirectional mobility between the V4 and a list of Eastern Partnership and Western Balkans countries: Albania, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Georgia, Kosovo, Moldova, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, and Ukraine. A citizen of one of these partner countries can study in a V4 country, and vice versa.

3

Because eligibility is tied to citizenship or long-term residence in the V4 or the specified partner countries, the Visegrad Scholarship Program is not open to applicants from outside this defined region - Pakistani nationals without residence or citizenship ties to a V4 or partner country are not eligible to apply. It is included here as a genuine, currently active regional scholarship for students who do hold citizenship or qualifying residence in one of the eligible countries, or for institutions/counsellors advising students from those regions.

4

Each scholar receives EUR 3,500 per semester, paid directly to the student, while the host institution receives an additional EUR 2,000 per semester to help cover administrative and academic support costs. All fields of study and any teaching language are accepted, provided the host university offers instruction and confirms admission in that language. Applications are submitted online through the Fund's 'My Visegrad' portal, alongside an acceptance letter from the host institution.

## Eligibility Requirements

5requirements

Must be a citizen or long-term resident of a V4 country (Czechia, Hungary, Poland, Slovakia) or an eligible Eastern Partnership/Western Balkans country (Albania, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Georgia, Kosovo, Moldova, Montenegro, North Macedonia, Serbia, Ukraine)

Must apply to study in a country different from your country of citizenship/residence, within the eligible V4-partner mobility directions

Hold a completed Bachelor's degree (for Master's-level mobility) or be enrolled in/have completed a Master's degree (for post-Master's/research mobility)

Secure an official letter of acceptance or invitation from the host institution before the deadline

Not have completed prior studies in the host country for the relevant degree level

## How to Apply - Step by Step

1

Step 1: Confirm your citizenship/residence and the host country fall within an eligible Visegrad mobility direction (Intra-Visegrad or V4-Eastern Partnership/Western Balkans).

2

Step 2: Contact your intended host university to secure an official acceptance or invitation letter.

3

Step 3: Register on the My Visegrad online application portal.

4

Step 4: Upload all required documents (transcripts, acceptance letter, ID, research plan if applicable) before April 15.

5

Step 5: Await the Fund's selection decision, typically announced by early summer for the autumn semester intake.

6

Step 6: Complete host-university enrollment and visa/residence formalities before the semester begins.

## Insider Tips & Pro Tips

TIP 1

Secure your host-university acceptance letter early - it is a hard requirement for the application and can take weeks to obtain

TIP 2

Only apply for mobility directions you are actually eligible for based on citizenship/residence; the Fund checks this strictly

TIP 3

Research-track (post-Master's) applicants should prepare a clear, concrete working plan rather than a general research interest statement

TIP 4

Because funding is fixed regardless of host city, factor cost of living into your choice of host institution/country

TIP 5

Apply as soon as the portal opens on January 1 rather than waiting for the April 15 deadline, since review can take time

TIP 6

Check the Fund's published statistics/success rates by country and field before applying to calibrate expectations
